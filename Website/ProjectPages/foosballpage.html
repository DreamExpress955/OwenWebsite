Autonomous Foosball Robot

PROJECT OVERVIEW

The Project

This project involved developing an autonomous robotic system capable of playing foosball using computer vision and embedded motor control. A camera captures the playing field while a Raspberry Pi processes the image data and determines the position and movement of the ball.

Movement commands are sent to an STM32 microcontroller, which controls the electromechanical systems responsible for positioning and rotating the foosball players.

The project was completed as a two-person engineering project over approximately five weeks and required the integration of mechanical, electrical, embedded, and software systems.

MECHANICAL DESIGN

Linear & Rotational Movement

The mechanical system required two distinct types of movement. Linear actuation positions the foosball players along the playing field, while rotational actuation allows the players to kick and interact with the ball.

Linear Positioning

A NEMA 17 stepper motor and custom mechanical components provide controlled linear positioning of the player assembly.

Rotational Control

A DC gear motor provides rotational movement for the player rod, allowing the system to perform kicking actions.

CONTROL DEVELOPMENT

Motor Control

The motor-control system was developed and tested to provide reliable positioning and rotational movement. MATLAB and control-system modelling were used during development alongside the STM32 embedded controller.

TESTING & RESULTS

Individual components were tested before the complete system was assembled. Testing included motor operation, mechanical movement, limit switches, communication, vision processing, and final system integration.

Ball Tracking

Camera-based processing was used to locate and follow the ball during gameplay.

Player Positioning

Linear movement positioned the player assembly based on commands from the control system.

Rotational Movement

The rotational motor system allowed automated player movement and kicking.

Safety Limits

Limit switches were incorporated to constrain physical movement and protect the mechanism.
